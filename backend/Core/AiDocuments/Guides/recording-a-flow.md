# Recording a flow

## What the recorder does

The recorder watches what you do once, lets you review it, and writes it into a flow. You are not
asked a question per action: each kind of action becomes steps by a rule, and you correct the
result in Review and afterwards in the editor.

A flow is created before recording starts: its name, what it should do, and what it works in - an
application's window, or a website's tab in a browser - which becomes its main area. Everything
recorded is kept relative to it. "This flow is a QA test" is off until AI is on, because a QA test
is generated by the model.

Two steps can go before what you record. One launches the application, or opens the browser at the
website when no tab has it. The other resizes the window, to a size you type or copy from one of its
open windows with **Measure its windows**: the whole window, title bar and borders included. Record
now and the bar's Start set that size too, so the recording is made at the size every execution
sets. A window that keeps its size - maximised, full screen, or one that cannot be resized - is said,
and nothing is recorded until it is restored or the step is unticked.

A website is found by its address when the recording starts, so have it open, in front in its
browser. From then on the recording follows that browser window, onto a sign-in on another site too.
An execution finds the tab by its address on every step, though, so steps on a page at another
address, such as that sign-in, need an area of their own for it.

## What is and is not recorded

Clicks, drags, scrolls and typing are recorded. A press and release become one click; a burst of
typing becomes one entry; a long gap becomes a pause.

Cursor movement on its own is not recorded. Recording every movement would produce thousands of
events in a couple of minutes and bury the ones that matter.

When you press a mouse button, StepinFlow keeps the window you picked as it was just before - the
screen you were looking at when you decided to click, rather than whatever the click then changed.

## The recording bar

Record now minimises StepinFlow and opens a small bar at the top of the screen. Arrange your windows,
then press Start on the bar. While recording, the bar shows whether it is recording or paused, a
clock, the last few actions, and Pause, Stage and Stop. Closing the bar before Start records nothing
and brings StepinFlow back. Nothing you do on the bar is recorded, and it never appears in a
screenshot. Pause and Stop also have keys, F9 and F10 by default, which work whatever window is in
front and are never recorded; they are under Recording keys in Settings. Stage is only on the bar.

Stage marks where a part of what you are testing begins - "Sign in", "Delete customer" - which is
the one thing no model can work out. Nothing is typed then: Review asks for its name. Typed text shows in the
bar as a count, "Typed 15 characters", never as the text.

## Marking a check or a step while recording

Two clicks mark something rather than click it, and never reach the application: **Ctrl + click**
marks a check on what you click, and **Ctrl + right click** a step that is not a click. Either
pauses the recording and opens a window over the application that asks what it is for. The window
takes the keyboard, so an application may close an open menu meanwhile; when it closes, the window
you were in gets the keyboard back.

- **A check** goes by its picture, or by its words and a rule they follow: contain, are exactly,
  match a pattern, are a number above, below or between, or are not empty. It waits until it is
  there or until it has gone. While the window is open, StepinFlow finds what you clicked and reads
  the words on it, a second or two, and says whether the rule passes on the screen as it is. It can
  tell Discord when it succeeds, when it fails, or both, each with a message of its own, through
  one of your bots or a new one made there; what the check found, or why it failed, goes with it.
- **A step** is a command, an application opened, a notification through a bot, an input's value
  typed, a wait of a set time, or a stage begun and named there. Where you right clicked does not
  matter, except to a notification with "Read text from the screen" ticked: it reads the text
  around where you right clicked and sends it with the message, or only the part a pattern keeps.
  The window shows the area it reads and what reads there now; you move and resize the area in
  Review. In the flow it is a Wait For Text on an area of its own, waiting until it reads anything,
  with the Notify and its result in the read's Success branch. When nothing reads in time, nothing
  is sent and the flow carries on. The read is named by the words there, not by the value it saw.

**Add and keep recording** keeps the answer; **Decide in Review** keeps none, and Review asks
instead. Either resumes the recording, and so do Resume and Stop, which leave it for Review. The
time spent in the window is not a wait the flow makes. Review starts from every answer and can
change it. Turn off "Ask at once" under Settings, Recording keys, for an application that cannot
lose the keyboard: a mark then only marks, and Review asks.

## What each click hit

When the recording stops, StepinFlow looks at the screenshot from before each click and finds the
element under it — the button, field or icon — and reads the text on it. It takes about a second
per click. Then it groups the clicks that hit the same picture, such as the same download icon on
several rows, and reads all the text on every screenshot to see what changed from one click to the
next - and from the last click to the screen as you left it when you pressed Stop, so let the last
screen finish loading first. The recording page shows how far each of those has got.

At each click, while recording, StepinFlow also asks Windows what the click landed on, as the
application describes it to screen readers: a button, a link, a text field, its name, and whether
it is enabled, ticked or a password field. A browser and most desktop applications answer; a game
draws its own pixels and does not. Windows only.

A box around the click stands in for the element when the model had nothing to say: no element was
under the click, the element was bigger than the area it looked at, or the model file is not
installed.

## Review

Every action is listed with a checkbox to keep it or leave it out. Select one to see the window at
that moment, where you clicked, and the rectangle the template will be cut from; drag its corners
to change it. Each action says what it becomes:

- **A click**: wait for it, then click (the default); click this exact spot without looking for
  anything; or leave it out. A click outside the window you picked starts left out.
- **Typed text**: an input, named from the field you clicked before typing, with what you typed as
  its default; or the text as written. A field Windows says is a password field, or that reads as
  one, makes the input secret, which is never written to a file: its value is typed once on the
  Inputs tab, and the flow does not start until it has one.
- **A key, a scroll**: kept, or left out.
- **A check you marked**: until it is there or until it has gone, by its picture or by its words and
  their rule, and what it tells Discord.
- **A step you marked**: which step, and what it needs - the command, the program, the bot and the
  message, the input, how long to wait, the stage's name.
- **A stage you marked**: its name. With none it is no stage. Where the screen showed a new page,
  Review offers to begin a stage there as well, and the list marks it "new page".

Beside it: the text read under the click, what Windows says it was, how long you waited before it,
and which other clicks hit the same picture. "What it should do" can be edited here too.

## The plain draft

"Plain draft, no AI" writes the reviewed recording into the flow and shows it on Check:

- A click becomes a search that waits for the element, then a move onto it and a click. The wait is
  three times how long you waited, never under 10 seconds nor over 60, with a comment saying what
  you waited.
- Everything after a search, up to the next search, goes in that search's Success branch. Its
  Failure branch ends the execution, failed, saying what was not found.
- Searches on one page sit beside each other. The first search on a new page goes in the Success
  branch of the search whose click opened it, after that click, and a page come back to - a dialog
  closed, a menu left - goes back beside the searches of that page. A stage you named starts at the
  top of the flow again.
- A search is named after what was pressed: as the application named it to Windows, such as "Find
  Username" for the Username field or "Find Open Menu" for a button, unless Windows only named
  what it sits in, a group or a page. Then by the words read on it, and when what was found is
  only a box around the click, by the whole line of words under the click, so a box that cuts
  through "Logout" still names it "Find Logout". "Find icon" when there are none.
- Clicks on the same picture in several places, one after another with nothing else between them,
  become a search that waits for the picture and a Find All Images that clicks every one on the
  screen: the download icon on every row. Clicks on one picture with something done between them,
  or the same place clicked again, stay clicks of their own. Clicks that hit the same picture
  always share one template.
- Typed text types its input, such as {{email}}. A pause is not a step; it sizes the next wait.
- A check is a wait for it, or for it to go; what came after it goes in its Success branch. Its
  notification when it succeeds is the first step there, and when it fails it comes before the
  execution ends, each `with result of` the check.
- A stage you named is `##` and its name over the next search, at the top of the flow.

The draft is written the way an imported script is, so a draft that does not read cleanly leaves the
flow as it was and says on which line.

## Generate flow

"Generate flow" writes the plain draft, then asks the model chosen in Settings for its words: for each
search, a name after the task, a comment saying what it is for, and what its failure says. It is off
until Ollama is running with a model chosen under Settings, AI.

The model never writes the script. It is sent what the flow should do and the searches as a list,
each with an id - S1, S2 - what the screen said there, and what was done once it was found, and it
answers by those ids. The app puts each answer on its step on its own: a name longer than a line, or
empty, keeps the draft's, a name already used is numbered, and the rest of the answer is still taken.
So nothing it says can add, drop or move a step you recorded, change a template or an input, or stop
the flow from saving. What was left out is listed on Check. With a small local model it takes tens of
seconds, since the request is a few thousand tokens and not the whole flow format. When no answer
reads, or the model cannot be reached, the plain draft is saved instead, and the page says why.

With it goes how a finished flow names its searches: one of the example flows that come with the
app, the one nearest what this flow is for, found by meaning with the embedding model the assistant
searches its guide with.

First, the local model looks at the screens, when it can take pictures: it says in a sentence which
page each screen is where a new page began, and names in a few words each click that had no words
on it and no name from Windows, such as an icon in a game. It is always the model in Ollama, even
when a cloud model writes the words, so the screenshots never leave this machine; only what it said
goes on. About two seconds a look on a graphics card, and much slower without one.

The notes under each search, starting "screen:", say what the screen said: where a new page began,
by its words or by the window's title, what was on it and what the local model said it is; what was
pressed, as the application named it to Windows or the local model saw it; and what appeared and
went after the click, read by OCR from the screenshots taken while recording. They are for the
model only, and never in the flow.

The model may also ask you something only you can answer, which Check shows.

In a QA test the model is asked a second time, once the searches have their names: where each part
of the test begins, as stages, and a check after a click for words that appeared - the button now
reads "Remove", "Thank you for your order" - which is what makes a replay a test. A check is taken
only for words a screenshot showed appearing after that click, since any other would fail the first
execution, and a stage only before a search where you began none. Whatever it says, a QA test has a
stage - named after the flow when nobody named one - and ends with End Execution passed. With "Let
the cloud model see your screen" off it proposes stages only.

The text read under each click and in the notes goes too, unless a cloud model answers and "Let the
cloud model see your screen" is off; then the model names the steps from what the flow should do,
and the notes say only where a new page began and what kind of thing was pressed - a button, a text
field - with no words, titles or names. What you typed never goes: the model sees only the input's
name, such as {{email}}. The screenshots and templates stay on this machine. "Show the exact
request" shows what was sent.

When a cloud model would be sent text read off your screen, the request is shown first, and nothing
leaves the machine until you press Send. The lines that hold such text are marked: the notes, and
every line with words read on what you clicked or the words you searched by. A line you wrote with
the same words is marked too. Cancel sends nothing; to send none of it, turn off "Let the cloud
model see your screen" under Settings, AI, and generate again - words you chose to search by still
go, and are still shown first. The local model is never held up, since nothing leaves the machine.

The page shows each stage as it happens. Once it is done, "Check the flow" shows what was saved.

## Check

Check shows the flow as it was saved, before it ever executes:

- **Script**: the flow as a script, numbered, its steps and branches folding. After a model, what its
  words changed is green, with the draft's lines it replaced in red above them, and a warning from
  the import is underlined at its line. **Changes from the draft** puts the draft and the saved flow
  side by side, with what did not change folded away.
- **Tree**: the steps as the editor shows them, every branch open.
- What the model did: its notes, what it asks you, and what of its answer was left out and why; for
  the plain draft, how it was written.
- **Checks the model added**, each with its line and the search it is under. They are in the flow
  already: **Keep** stops offering it, and **Drop** takes it out, with what it did when it failed,
  while the steps under it stay where it was. A check another step reads what it found from is not
  dropped here.
- The errors and warnings: what the import let through, at its line, and what the flow's validation
  says.

**Ask for changes** sends the script back to the model with what you write, such as "also sign out
at the end". Unlike Generate, the model writes the whole script here, so a small local model often
fails at it. The answer is read and checked the way an imported file is, three times at most, and
the checks you have not kept or dropped yet are still offered after it. When one reads cleanly it
replaces the flow, and Check marks what it changed; when none does, the flow is as it was and Check
says why. The model cannot cut a template, so a change that needs a search for something no template
shows is left out, and its notes say so. The script goes as the flow holds it, step names included,
even with "Send text read from the screen" off.

**Open in the editor** goes on to the flow. **Discard the flow** deletes it, and the recording with
it.

## Recording into a flow you already have

Not available yet: the editor's **Record steps here** button is disabled until a model's draft can
go into a flow that already exists. Record a new flow instead, and call it from the existing one as
a sub-flow.
